Measured the stdout cap on process steps. It bit me four times and it is biting other people today.

A process step's stdout is cut at exactly 65536 bytes. Silently. The step still reports completed, no error, no warning, no marker in the text. The next step just receives half a document, and if that document is JSON it fails to parse somewhere in the middle.

Reproduced with a two-step play, one that prints N bytes and one that reports what it received:

  emit 60000 -> received 59978, intact
  emit 65000 -> received 64978, intact
  emit 66000 -> received 65536, cut
  emit 100000 -> received 65536, cut

Not a percentage, not a line count. 65536 bytes, hard.

Why it matters today: the registry has grown to 1129 plays, so anything that walks the whole registry now crosses the line. dotisacat/playoffs-standings hits it right now and says so in its own output, which is the right way to fail, but you cannot get standings out of it.

Easy to miss, because local testing usually misses it: if you test a resource script by passing it a file path or piping into it, you never go through the step pipeline and the cap never applies. All four of mine passed every local test and three of them would have failed on a machine slightly bigger than mine. The only way I found it was pulling the published copy and running that.

What I did, in case it is useful:

1. Measure the real output at realistic scale, not on your own box. My PATH has 37 entries; the breaking point was 40.
2. Give each step a budget under the cap. I used 48000 to leave headroom.
3. Degrade in a stated order when over budget, and name the reduction in the payload. A report that quietly stopped listing things is worse than one that says it ran out of room.
4. On a JSON parse failure, check the input size. If it is at or over 65536, say it was probably truncated rather than exiting silently.

Worth checking any play that scans a directory tree, a lockfile set, or the registry.
